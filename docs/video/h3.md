# MiniMax H3: what making films with it taught

What the H3 project learned over a few thousand clips (its LESSONS, character rules, node-features and runbook
notes), and what moving it into nextsycl added, as the rules the tools now follow and why. The prompt format
itself is the model's guide (`model-cards/guides/minimax-h3.md`, `video.describePromptGuideMD`); a whole film made
through the API alone is `tests/api/kaiju_lighthouse.py`. The guide's "Films" section turns this into a recipe
for each kind of film - a monologue, an action scene, a montage; this page says why. What to carry across each
kind of cut (a new speaker, a new place, a match cut ...) is [scenes.md](scenes.md).

The general rule under all of it, learned the hard way: **when something must be remembered at the call site to
avoid silently wrong output, the default is the bug** - make the tool read the answer from the data. (The join
overlap below was written down, then forgotten on the next three films, each shipping a replayed second at every
cut.)

## Carrying a clip on: motion, sound, picture

| What carries | How | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| a picture | `first_frame` (a PNG, or a clip: its **last frame**, a still) | the pose and the look - not the motion |
| motion | `guide_clip: "<clip>:22:0"` - its last 22 frames as one moving keyframe at frame 0 | position *and* velocity |
| sound | `first_audio: "<clip>"`, `first_audio_s` - its sound tail (`.lastaud`, the model's own level) | room tone, score, a voice mid-line |

- **A single keyframe pins a pose, not a velocity.** Measured over six cuts, the frames either side match at
  35.6 dB PSNR at the cut and fall to 32.0 / 29.9 / 28.2 / 24.4 / 22.8 dB 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 / 12 frames in: heads
  jump at the seams. A clip given as `first_frame` is the same single keyframe (the engine reads its last frame).
- **The fix is the moving guide**: 22 frames (0.92 s; guide lengths are 17k + 5 - 5, 22, 39 - and longer ones are
  cut down) plus a 0.9167 s audio anchor, so both streams cover the same span. (H3 planned this pairing but held
  the audio anchor to at least 1 s, two frames more sound than picture; the floor here is 0.75 s - the audio
  encoder fails below ~0.6 s.) The next clip then **begins with**
  those 22 frames and that sound again: make it 0.92 s longer to keep the beat's length, and join with the
  overlap inside the guided frames (below). With a guide, give no `first_frame` - the guide's last frame is that
  picture already, and a picture at frame 0 as well pins frame 0 to two different frames.
- **Without a guide, the audio anchor fights the keyframe**: a 1 s anchor forces a 1 s overlap at the join (12
  frames trimmed each side), which throws away the pinned frames and joins on 23 dB frames. So: no audio anchor
  and a 2-frame cross-fade (~30 dB).
- **Chain a PNG, never the last latent.** A latent slice is not what encoding a picture gives (the VAE squeezes
  time, and its scale differs): the next clip jumps lighter or darker, with false texture.
- **Drift compounds along a chain**: each decode / encode hop darkens the anchor by about 1 luma unit - an 18-clip
  chain ended 19% darker. `exposure_ref` (clip 1's last frame) rescales each picture keyframe to its luminance
  (correcting drift, never re-grading: the gain is held to 0.75-1.35). It applies to `first_frame` / `last_frame`
  pictures, not to guide frames.
- **A hub beats a chain**: every clip opens on clip 1's last frame and closes on it too (`last_frame: first`;
  pinned ends land at 33.8 dB, as exactly as the opening; 12 frames before the end it is still converging,
  26 dB), so nothing compounds. The price: each clip must end back in its opening pose and framing. With a guide in
  a hub, the guide (and the sound) is clip 1's end as well.
- **Never `cond_noise_aug`** (nor its audio twin): the keyframe latent is never denoised, so noise added to it
  decodes as coloured confetti (seen at 0.03) or is heard.
- **The audio anchor has repeated words** across cuts; for one steady voice across a film, a voice reference on
  every clip (`ref_audios`, `<Audio 1>`) is the steadier lock - and once the voice comes from a reference, drop
  the timbre adjectives from the prompt.
- **Framing creeps**: "pushes in slightly" compounded over six chained clips into a close-up. Lock the shot size
  in positive words ("that exact shot size and camera position hold from the first frame to the last"). Two
  cameras cut together read as jarring.
- **The turbo 4-step ref2v LoRA** halves sampling but breaks keyframe adherence; the subject drifts out of frame
  over 15 s.

What the tools do with this: `nextsycl video speech` anchors in a hub (both ends), chains PNGs, matches exposure,
and either uses no audio anchor or, with `--guide-frames 22`, the guide's clip's sound for 22/24 s with no
picture at frame 0; the API test chains each clip from the last with a guide and its sound.

## Frame planning

Plan a film in frames, not seconds: the engine makes every clip 17k + 5 frames (round(seconds x 24), then up to
the next such count - ask 10.92 s, get 277 frames, 11.54 s), and whatever a clip carries on is replayed at the
start of the next.

    clip 1          f1 = 17k + 5 >= its beat x 24            (10 s: 243 frames, 10.125 s)
    clip i > 1      fi = the 17k + 5 nearest to (f1 + (i-1) x beat x 24) - film so far + g
    film so far     f1 + sum over later clips of (fi - g)    (g: the carried frames, 22 for the guide)
    ask for         fi / 24 seconds (exact, so it rounds to fi)

With 10 s beats and a 22-frame guide: 243, 260, 260 frames -> a 719-frame film, 29.96 s. The overlap at each join
is g frames of picture (cut in the middle: 11 trimmed each side) and g / 24 s of sound (`first_audio_s`, cross-
faded over all of it); size the audio anchor to the guide, or the sound and picture of the carried span disagree
by the difference. The step after the frame count is memory: a later clip is g frames longer than its beat, which
is what pushes 768x768 past INT8 (Q6_K fits it).

## Joining

- **The overlap is the carried span, read from the clips' records**: an audio anchor of s seconds or a guide of N
  frames means the next clip replays that much - trim half of it each side (k = round(s x 24 / 2), 11 for the
  22-frame guide) and cross-fade the sound over all of it. A plain concat plays it twice. `nextsycl video join`
  reads `first_audio_s` and `guide_clip` from each job's record; nothing to pass.
- **Frame-exact**: each clip's sound padded or trimmed to frames / 24 (it drifted ~30 ms a clip, 3.5 s over 103
  clips) and the result checked within a frame.
- **Level by loudness, -18 LUFS**: per-clip peak normalisation gave gains of 1.6-5.2x and an ambience bed that
  swells at every cut. Level each clip in a film of like clips (speech); a film whose score builds is levelled as a
  whole (the API test), or its build-up is flattened. A limiter after.
- **Fade out 1.5 s.** Denoise is a bandage (afftdn nr 12 is gentle, 24 strong, past ~30 the voice dulls): fix the
  soundscape instead.

## Prompts (beyond the format)

- cfg is 1.0: **every word renders**, negations included ("no alarm" made the alarm). Say what is there.
- **The word count sets the speech rate**, not the clip's length: the model speaks for about 57% of any clip. 22
  words is clear (3.3 a second), 28 the cap, 34 too fast (4.7). Clip = words / rate / 0.57, 4-15 s, snapped to
  17k + 5 frames; a clip under 45% speech is flagged. (The older "(seconds - 2) x 3.3" over-fills long clips.)
- **The soundscape is rendered under the whole clip**: weather, traffic, electrical beds come back as hiss, a hum
  becomes music. Name only sounds something in the shot makes.
- Say **blinking** (plural for two); assert the **eyeline** about three times; hold the **light level and colour**
  "from the first frame to the last"; say the room is **empty**, or background people re-roll.
- **Motion**: count the clauses - whichever side has more wins; bound an amplitude with a distance ("a hand's
  width"); say who moves. Ask a voice for power and projection, not depth (pitch-shifting in post failed).
- One clear action a shot, at most ~3 shots in 10 s; timeline beats as `At MM:SS.mmm`.

## Size, memory, quality

- **Memory is tokens**, which grow with width x height x seconds (and the sound). On a 32 GB card (30 GiB for the
  kernels): INT8 fits 768x576 x 15 s (~48k tokens) and 768x768 x 10 s (~43k); it does not fit 768x768 x 15 s
  (64k), 768x768 x 10.9 s with a 22-frame guide, nor 896x672 / 1024x768 / 576x1024 at 15 s; 1344x768 fits 5 s.
  Q6_K keeps INT8's composition in 4 GiB less (Q4_K_M drifts further). There is a step-time cliff at ~21.9k tokens
  (+30%). A clip that does not fit fails at the denoiser ("a scratch buffer ... would pass the device memory cap")
  and the studio **pauses its whole queue** (`video.queue.resume`).
- **The house recipe**: 768x576, 8 steps (a draft; 12-20 when dialogue or motion matter), the teacher (32B) text
  encoder (the 4B student speaks nonsense), upscale 1.5 (1152x864). The realism LoRA at 0.5 for live-action people
  (1.0 overrides the framing into a close-up; its trigger word barely matters), none for drawn characters.
- **Upscaling**: ESRGAN general-x4v3 on the decoded frames is the default - sharp and natural, about half the
  decode's time (17.9 s against 41.9 s at 1152x864). The latent upscaler keeps more of the decoder's texture,
  needs the pack's 3d backbone (2d decodes to noise) and was trained mostly at 2.0.
- Trained lengths: 124-362 frames (5.2-15.1 s). A clip's frames are always 17k + 5 (the seconds asked, times 24,
  rounded up to one): ask for 10.92 s and get 277 frames, 11.54 s - plan a chain in frames.

## Working

- **Draft small, then render once**: one 1.6 s clip (~2 min) tests a look; one clip plus its speech share and a
  contact sheet before a series; a whole film at 384x384 x 4 s x 8 steps (~4 min) tests the story, the prompts
  and the chain. A draft does **not** carry its composition to the final: the noise is drawn over the latent's
  shape, so the same seed at another size or length is another picture. Re-roll at the final size.
- Re-rendering one bad clip of a hub is safe (nothing depends on it but the join); delete the old one's mp4 and
  record first, or the joiner may take either.
- Through the API: `video.upload` stores the file under a new name (`ref_<time>_<name>`) - use the name it
  answers. `"prev"` means the series' last finished clip, and **with none it falls back to the newest clip
  anywhere** - so chain a series by the clip's file name once it is done (the API test does), not by queueing
  `prev` ahead. `video.timeline {project}` says when a clip is done and its name; `GET /v1/video/files/<file>`
  fetches it.
- Bugs that show only in the daemon, from the second job on, are cross-job cached state: a reorder cache keyed by
  device pointer once gave a later clip's video encoder the previous job's upscaler weights (washed-out keyframes
  under a mesh). Test with two jobs in one fresh daemon.

## Open

- `te_pictures` (the keyframe pictures shown to the text encoder, on by default as in ComfyUI) is new against the
  H3 project's films: whether it helps or hurts continuity across chained clips is untested.
- Seeds: every tool uses one seed for every clip; no per-clip seed strategy has been measured.
- H3's own builds reported byte-identical latents for the same inputs; nextsycl measured run-to-run cosine 0.996.
  One test should settle which holds.
- Exposure matching of guide frames (only pictures are rescaled today).
